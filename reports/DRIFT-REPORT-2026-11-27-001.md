## Suite B — framediff media check (Two Lights v1 vs v3)

**Pinned baseline (2026-11-26):** `framediff.py`, window 6.5–10.0 s
excluded, 156 frames compared outside the window. Self-diff (v3 vs
v3): MAD max/mean/median 0.0 / 0.0 / 0.0. Cross-diff (v3 vs v1):
MAD max 78.6012, mean 68.6311, median 70.1647; 156 of 156 frames
over MAD 5.

**Re-run (2026-11-27):** the same harness (sha256 `bd9ed544…`) on
the same two files (hash-pinned in the records). Every statistic
reproduced **exactly** — delta 0.0000 on max, mean and median, for
both the self-diff and the cross-diff. The instrument did not drift.

**What moved — the subject did.** Between Two Lights v1 and v3,
**all 156 frames outside the retake window differ substantially**:
mean absolute pixel difference 68.63 on a 0–255 scale, median
70.16, minimum frame still over MAD 5 — every single one. The v3
retake did not preserve the earlier version's frames outside the
window; the change between versions is total, not local. That is
the change this suite exists to catch, measured the day the bench
was built, on the bench's own first subject.

**Not measured:** the 84 frames inside the 6.5–10.0 s window are
excluded by the harness's design; their change is assigned to
visual and human checks in the Fix the Take spec, whose paid Retake
firing has not taken place. MAD is a pixel statistic — it does not
say which version is *better*, only that they differ, and by how
much.

## Limitations of this report

- Suite A's live-app arm is inconclusive (above); only the
  certified-checker arm was re-measured.
- Suite B's window frames are unmeasured by design, and MAD is a
  pixel statistic, not a quality judgement.
- Suite C proves integrity, not content quality.
- Baselines were transcribed from records made on Nov 17 and
  Nov 26; the re-runs are this run's own measurements. Where a
  baseline's precision was 2 decimal places, agreement is claimed
  at that precision.
- Nothing here certifies any subject as good, safe or correct. It
  records what changed, what did not, and what could not be
  measured — which is the whole of the claim.

## What this proves, and what it does not

It proves the bench works end to end on three different kinds of
subject — a tool, a model output, a skill pack — and that it can
tell the three apart: a total change (B), exact stability (A, C),
and an honest could-not-measure (A's app arm). It does not yet meet
the smallest proof's pass line: step 4 (the runner in five outside
hands, run twice) has not happened. That step is next, and it needs
people, not code.
